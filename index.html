Lateral Thinking Lab

Logical thinking moves step by step in a straight line. Lateral thinking steps to the side and asks, “What if I look at this a different way?” In this lab, your group of three will practice both: finding creative answers, then giving clear reasons for them.

Four moves for thinking sideways

When you are stuck, try one of these moves. Use them in every part of the lab.

Change the lensLook at meaning, then grammar, then sound, then spelling, then use.
Question the assumptionWhat did you believe without checking? Is it really true?
Reverse itStart from the answer, or from the opposite, and work back.
Ask “what else?”Your first idea is a start, not the finish. Find two more.

For each set, click the word that doesn’t belong, then finish the sentence. Careful: more than one answer can be defended.

The point goes to the best reason, not the “right” answer. Use the three checks to test your reason before you look at other possible answers.

Challenge: argue the other side

Pick one set and defend a different answer than your group chose. The Skeptic leads this one.

What is it? Collocation riddles

Handout B, Part 2

A collocation is a group of words that often go together, like make a mistake or catch a bus. A collocation riddle describes a secret word through the words that go with it.

First, solve the riddles below. Then write your own, with the hardest clue first.

Solve: model riddles and practice

Write your own

Start your hard clue with a collocation list: “You can ___ it, ___ it, or ___ it.” Make each clue a little easier than the one before.

What else could it be?

New: alternative uses challenge

Choose one everyday object. In three minutes, list as many different uses for it as your group can. Forget what it is “for.” A brick can be a doorstop, a paperweight, or a tiny step for a short person.

Speaker says ideas fast, Scribe types one idea per line, Skeptic does not judge until the timer ends.

Yes/No mysteries

New: situation puzzles

Each story sounds strange. Your job is to find the hidden explanation by asking yes/no questions only.

One person is the Keeper: they secretly read the answer and reply “Yes,” “No,” or “Not important.” The other two are Askers. Change the Keeper for every mystery. Fewer questions is better.
